French electrical authorisations: what do B0, BR, B2V and BC mean?

23 November 20263 min read

Key points

  • In France, the electrical authorisation ("habilitation électrique") is issued by the employer, after training: it recognises that a worker can safely carry out the electrical tasks assigned to them.
  • The French Labour Code requires it for any operation on an electrical installation or in its vicinity; the NF C 18-510 standard sets the rules.
  • Its symbol is read character by character: the voltage range, the type of operation, then a detail if needed.

At an event in France, not everyone may open an electrical cabinet. The French Labour Code is clear: operations on electrical installations or in their vicinity may only be carried out by authorised workers (article R. 4544-9).

Who issues the authorisation?

The employer. INRS, the French institute for occupational risk prevention, defines it as the recognition, by the employer, of the ability of a person under their authority to carry out the tasks assigned to them safely with regard to electrical risk. It follows training, and the employer, or their delegate, signs the authorisation certificate.

It is not enough on its own: to carry out an operation, the authorised worker must also be designated by their employer.

Reading a symbol

The NF C 18-510 standard builds each authorisation symbol with two or three characters.

  • 1st character, the voltage range: B for low and extra-low voltage, H for high voltage.
  • 2nd character, the type of operation: 0 for non-electrical work, 1 for an operative, 2 for a person in charge of works, R for a general intervention, S for a basic intervention, C for a lockout.
  • 3rd character, a detail: V for the reinforced vicinity zone, T for live working.

B2V therefore reads: low voltage, person in charge of electrical works, including in the reinforced vicinity zone. The vicinity is the space close to a bare live part, where prevention measures are required.

Who does what?

  • B0: no electrical operation, but access to areas with an electrical risk.
  • BS: basic interventions on final circuits, 400 V and 32 A AC at most, such as replacing a lamp or a fuse like for like, or resetting a protective device.
  • BR: maintenance and troubleshooting, from fault finding to replacing faulty equipment. A BR may connect and disconnect with voltage present under conditions: 500 V and 63 A AC at most, off load.
  • B1 and B2: works, such as creating or modifying an installation, or replacing an enclosure or a cabinet. B1 carries out the work; B2 is in charge of the works: they mark out the work area and check that the work is properly done.
  • BC: the lockout of an installation, one of the steps in making a circuit safe.

An authorisation is kept up to date

The employer sets how often refresher training takes place. INRS recommends 3 years, less when the practice is occasional.

The voltage, the role, the detail: three characters are enough to know who can do what on an installation.
