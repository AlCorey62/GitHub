HVO: what does this renewable fuel change for a generator?

16 November 20263 min read

Key points

  • HVO is a diesel fuel made by hydrotreating oils and fats. In France, it is called "gazole XTL" and must meet the NF EN 15940 standard.
  • French rules only allow it in a compatible engine: for a generator, what counts is the approval of the engine manufacturer.
  • According to the Base Carbone of ADEME, the French environment agency, it emits 77 to 82% less greenhouse gas than non-road diesel over its life cycle, for the same energy, depending on the raw material.

HVO, for Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil, is a renewable fuel made from oils and fats, for example used cooking oils or animal fats. Treated with hydrogen, they give a synthetic diesel that takes the place of diesel in the tank.

What the rules say in France

In France, this fuel is called "gazole XTL". Its characteristics are set by the order of 28 February 2017, amended by the order of 26 June 2024: it must meet the NF EN 15940:2023 standard, which covers paraffinic diesel fuels from synthesis or hydrotreatment.

Since the 2024 order, its use is no longer limited to captive fleets: it can be sold at filling stations, with specific labelling on the pumps. The text sets one condition: gazole XTL may only be used in vehicles compatible with this fuel, whose list is published in the French Official Journal.

And for a generator?

That list covers road vehicles. For a generator, the same logic applies: HVO only goes into an engine whose manufacturer accepts a fuel meeting NF EN 15940. Compatibility is therefore checked generator by generator, before the first fill.

What gain on emissions?

The gain is counted over the whole life cycle of the fuel, and it depends on the raw material. The Base Carbone of ADEME, the French public database of emission factors, gives these values, without land-use change, per unit of energy contained in the fuel:

Fuelkg CO₂e per GJDifference
Non-road diesel89.4reference
HVO 100, used cooking oils15.8−82%
HVO 100, animal fats20.7−77%

HVO does not replace the other levers. The first one remains connecting the event to the grid when possible, then not oversizing generators: a generator too big for its load burns fuel for nothing and clogs up.

HVO takes the place of diesel, but only in an engine that accepts it: compatibility is checked before the fill.
