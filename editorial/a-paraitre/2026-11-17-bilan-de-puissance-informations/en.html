Power assessment: what information to gather before asking for a study?

17 November 20263 min read

Key points

  • A power assessment starts from the list of your equipment: quantity and unit power of each item, in watts.
  • Two details change the result: the connection, single-phase or three-phase, and the diversity factor, the share of equipment running at the same time.
  • The supply available on site completes the picture: three-phase 400 V or single-phase 230 V.

Before studying a power distribution, our engineering office starts from a power assessment: the inventory of the event's equipment, with its power, its type of connection and its diversity factor. Five pieces of information are enough to start it.

The five pieces of information

  1. The equipment list, area by area, with quantities.
  2. The unit power of each item, in watts, read on its rating plate or data sheet, with its cos φ when stated.
  3. The connection: single-phase or three-phase. Single-phase equipment is then spread across the three phases to balance them.
  4. The diversity factor: 1 if all the equipment on a line runs at full power at the same time, less otherwise.
  5. The site supply: three-phase 400 V or single-phase 230 V.

Missing a power rating? If in doubt, our teams draw on field data gathered since 2016.

A worked example

Take ten 1,000 W appliances on single-phase 230 V, with a cos φ of 1 (assumption for the example) and the 20% power reserve offered by default in our online power assessment:

  • if they all run at the same time (diversity factor 1): 10 kVA, 43.5 A, or 52.2 A with the reserve. You need a single-phase P17 63 A socket and a 12 kVA source;
  • if six at most run at the same time (diversity factor 0.6): 6 kVA, 26.1 A, or 31.3 A with the reserve. A single-phase P17 32 A is enough, with a 7.2 kVA source.

Same equipment list, but a rating half the size: the diversity factor weighs as much as the power ratings. It must therefore stay realistic, because if it is too low, the installation is undersized.

What the study adds

The assessment gives guidance: power to plan for, current on each phase, indicative rating and source. It does not replace a study: installation conditions, selectivity, operating modes and load criticality still need to be checked.

The equipment list, its power ratings, its connection, its diversity factor and the site supply: with these five pieces of information, the study can start.
