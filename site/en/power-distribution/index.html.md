## You don't power a trade show the way you power a concert.

Every event is unique, and so is its configuration. Event power distribution means starting from the venue's main supply (or supplies) and distributing electricity to the places the organiser asks for, in compliance with safety standards.

### Shows, concerts and live events

The distribution cabinets sit behind the stage. Ideally, each department has its own source, with its own cabinet or cabinets, to avoid “buzz” and other electrical interference caused by the electronics in some equipment.

### Trade shows, fairs and exhibitions

Each stand must have its own power box fitted with a 30 mA RCD to protect people. Running an extension lead from the neighbouring stand, known in France as *ilotage*, is refused every time by safety officers. A trade show with 300 powered stands means 300 power boxes.

[Why *ilotage* is banned](https://www.darkside-energy.com/en/did-you-know/event-power-distribution/)

## From design to derig.

1. **Site survey and requirements**: Site visit, requirements gathering and power assessment.
2. **Design and plans**: Choice of sources, distribution diagram and site layout plan by our engineering office.
3. **Installation**: Installing cabinets, boxes and cables, in line with standards and the approved plans.
4. **Show**: Electrical standby during the event, generator monitoring and call-outs.
5. **Derig**: Taking down and packing up the equipment at the end of the event.

## When even the briefest micro-interruption is not an option.

Two synchronised generators run at the same time, each supplying 50% of the load. If one fails, the other takes over and, because it is already running, there is no interruption at all: consoles, computers and control rooms don't notice a thing.

[The Twin zero-interruption generator set explained](https://www.darkside-energy.com/en/did-you-know/twin-generators/)
